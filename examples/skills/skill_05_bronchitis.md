# TCM Diagnostic Inquiry Skill: Chronic Bronchitis (Cough / Phlegm-Dampness)

## When to Use This Skill

Use when generating TCM diagnostic dialogues for chronic bronchitis with
acute exacerbation, especially in elderly patients with recurrent winter
cough and sputum production.

## Core Disease Knowledge

- **TCM Disease Categories**: Chronic Cough (Jiu Ke), Phlegm-Retention (Tan Yin)
- **Etiology**: External pathogens triggering latent phlegm-dampness; Spleen
  deficiency failing to transform fluids, producing internal phlegm.
- **Pathomechanism**: Spleen is the source of phlegm, Lung is the container.
  Spleen deficiency → fluid retention → phlegm-dampness → obstruction of
  Lung Qi → cough and sputum.

## Key Differentiation Points

### Distinguishing from Asthma (Xiao Zheng)
- Bronchitis: cough and sputum are primary, wheezing is secondary or absent
- Asthma: episodic wheezing with rales, often allergic/atopic history

### Distinguishing from COPD (Fei Zhang)
- COPD: persistent dyspnea, barrel chest, irreversible airflow limitation
- Bronchitis: productive cough without significant emphysema

## Standard Inquiry Logic

Cough specialty inquiry (onset, duration, seasonality) → Sputum characteristics
(color, volume, consistency, ease of expectoration) → Etiology (external
contraction vs. internal injury triggers) → Accompanying respiratory symptoms
(dyspnea, wheezing, chest tightness) → Systemic symptoms (fatigue, appetite,
body heaviness — Spleen indicators) → Smoking and occupational exposure →
Physical examination and imaging correlation → Lung-Spleen-Kidney zang-fu
differentiation → Asthma and COPD differential → Critical screening (respiratory
failure, cor pulmonale signs) → TCM syndrome diagnosis.
