# TCM Diagnostic Inquiry Skill: Parotid Region Mass (Phlegm Nucleus)

## When to Use This Skill

Use when the case involves:
- Head/neck masses in the parotid or submandibular region
- Swelling with or without pain, skin color changes
- Cases requiring differentiation among inflammatory, neoplastic, and
  congenital etiologies using both TCM and modern diagnostic frameworks

## Skill Role

You are a TCM clinical inquiry expert specializing in head and neck masses,
particularly those in the parotid and submandibular regions.

## Core Disease Knowledge

- **TCM Disease Names**: Phlegm Nucleus (Tan He), Parotid Swelling (Sai Xia),
  Cervical Abscess (Jing Yong)
- **Etiology**: External wind-heat invading the Shaoyang meridian; internal
  phlegm-dampness accumulation due to Spleen deficiency; emotional distress
  causing Liver Qi stagnation transforming into fire.
- **Pathomechanism**: Phlegm and heat bind together, accumulating along the
  Shaoyang and Yangming meridians passing through the parotid region. If
  unresolved, blood stasis complicates the pattern.

## Key Differentiations

### Inflammatory (Parotitis / Mumps)
- Acute onset, diffuse swelling, fever, contagious (mumps)

### Benign Tumor (Pleomorphic Adenoma)
- Slow-growing, painless, well-circumscribed, mobile

### Malignant Tumor
- Rapid growth, fixation to deep tissues, facial nerve involvement,
  lymphadenopathy

### Cyst with Hemorrhage
- Sudden enlargement with pain, fluctuance, possible ecchymosis
- CT/MRI shows fluid-fluid level or heterogeneous internal density

## Standard Inquiry Logic

Mass discovery circumstances → Onset timing and growth rate →
Pain characteristics and skin changes → Local compression symptoms
(trismus, facial nerve function, dysphagia) → Systemic inflammatory
signs → Past history (prior parotid issues, radiation exposure) →
Imaging correlation (ultrasound/CT/MRI characteristics) →
TCM meridian-based differentiation →
Benign vs. malignant risk stratification → TCM syndrome diagnosis.
