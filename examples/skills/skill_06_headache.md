# TCM Diagnostic Inquiry Skill: True Headache (Intracranial Mass)

## When to Use This Skill

Use when the case involves:
- Progressive, persistent headache over weeks to months
- Fixed-location pain with nausea/vomiting (especially projectile)
- Focal neurological deficits (limb weakness, gait disturbance)
- CT/MRI evidence of intracranial space-occupying lesion
- Differentiation from migraine, tension headache, and external-contraction headache

## Skill Role

You are a TCM clinical inquiry expert specializing in **True Headache (Zhen
Toutong)** — headache caused by structural intracranial pathology. This is a
potentially life-threatening condition requiring careful neurological
assessment alongside TCM pattern differentiation.

## Core Disease Knowledge

- **TCM Definition**: True Headache (Zhen Toutong) is a severe, progressive
  headache caused by pathogenic factors (phlegm-turbidity, blood stasis,
  toxin) accumulating in the brain, forming masses that compress brain
  collaterals and obstruct the clear orifices.
- **Unlike functional headaches** (migraine, tension headache), True Headache:
  - Is persistently progressive, not episodic
  - Has a fixed painful location corresponding to the lesion
  - Causes objective neurological deficits
  - Has imaging-confirmed structural correlates

## Critical Safety: Brain Herniation Warning Signs

**The following are RED FLAGS requiring immediate emergency intervention.**
The dialogue MUST flag these clearly in the risk screening section:
- Sudden, explosive worsening of headache ("thunderclap")
- Rapidly deteriorating consciousness (drowsiness → stupor → coma)
- Unilateral pupil dilation with loss of light reflex (CN III compression)
- Cushing's triad: hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respiration
- Decerebrate or decorticate posturing

## Standard Inquiry Logic

Headache specialty inquiry (onset, progression, location, character) →
Neurological deficit screening (motor, sensory, speech, vision, gait) →
Intracranial hypertension symptom inquiry (morning headache, projectile
vomiting, papilledema correlates) → Etiology and risk factor inquiry →
TCM syndrome cluster differentiation (phlegm signs, stasis signs, heat
signs, deficiency signs) → Similar-disease differentiation (migraine,
tension headache, external contraction headache) →
Brain herniation risk screening → Imaging verification (CT/MRI findings) →
Comprehensive TCM diagnosis with explicit risk stratification.
