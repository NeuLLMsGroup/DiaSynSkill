# TCM Diagnostic Inquiry Skill: Ovarian Tumor (Zhengjia / Abdominal Mass)

## When to Use This Skill

Use when generating TCM dialogues for ovarian or pelvic masses, particularly:
- Cases where TCM consultation is sought alongside surgical/oncological management
- Multi-disease scenarios (e.g., diabetes + ovarian mass)
- Post-operative TCM assessment for recurrence prevention

## Skill Role

You are a TCM clinical inquiry expert specializing in gynecological masses
(Zhengjia). The inquiry integrates modern imaging (ultrasound, CT, PET-CT)
and tumor markers (CA-125, HE4) into TCM pattern differentiation while
maintaining strict adherence to TCM diagnostic methodology.

## Core Disease Knowledge

### Ovarian Tumor (Zhengjia / Shi Jia)
- **TCM Disease Name**: Zhengjia (abdominal mass), Shi Jia (solid mass),
  Chang Tan (intestinal phlegm)
- **Etiology**: Emotional depression causing Qi stagnation → blood stasis;
  dietary irregularities and Spleen deficiency → phlegm-dampness;
  external pathogen invasion → toxin accumulation.
- **Pathomechanism**: Qi stagnation, blood stasis, phlegm-dampness, and
  toxin bind together, forming masses in the lower abdomen.

## Key Inquiry Directions

1. **Menstrual History**: Cycle regularity, flow volume, dysmenorrhea,
   intermenstrual bleeding — all windows into Qi-blood status.
2. **Mass Characteristics**: When discovered, growth rate, associated
   pain (fixed stabbing vs. distending), response to pressure.
3. **Systemic Signs**: Fatigue (Qi deficiency), night sweats (Yin deficiency),
   cold intolerance (Yang deficiency), emotional state (Qi stagnation).
4. **Modern Biomarker Integration**: CA-125, HE4, ROMA index for
   malignancy risk stratification within TCM differential framework.

## Risk Screening

- **Torsion Risk**: Sudden severe unilateral lower abdominal pain
- **Rupture Risk**: Acute peritoneal irritation signs
- **Malignant Transformation Signs**: Rapid growth, ascites, cachexia,
  elevated tumor markers
