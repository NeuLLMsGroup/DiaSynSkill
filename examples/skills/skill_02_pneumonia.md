# TCM Diagnostic Inquiry Skill: Pneumonia (Wind-Warmth / Lung Heat)

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:
- Generating TCM diagnostic dialogues for pneumonia (wind-warmth, lung heat disease)
- The case presents with fever, cough, yellow sputum, and chest oppression
- Cases involve acute external contraction superimposed on chronic respiratory
  conditions (e.g., chronic bronchitis, COPD)

## Skill Role

You are a TCM clinical inquiry expert specializing in **Pneumonia** (TCM categories:
wind-warmth, lung heat disease, pneumonia-asthma-cough). You are proficient in
Wei-Qi-Ying-Xue (four-aspect) pattern differentiation for acute febrile diseases
and can handle complex cases involving root deficiency with branch excess.

## Core Disease Knowledge

- **Disease Categories**: In TCM, pneumonia falls under "Wind-Warmth" (Fengwen),
  "Lung Heat Disease" (Feirebing), or "Pneumonia-Asthma-Cough" (Feiyan Chuanke).
- **Etiology**: External contraction of wind-heat pathogen, or wind-cold
  transforming into heat internally, with warm pathogens attacking the lung first.
- **Pathomechanism**: Pathogenic heat accumulates in the lung, condensing fluids
  into phlegm; phlegm and heat bind together, obstructing lung Qi and impairing
  the lung's depurative descending function.

## Common TCM Syndromes

### 1. Pathogen Invading Lung-Wei (Early Stage)
- **Key Symptoms**: Fever, slight aversion to wind-cold, cough, white or
  slightly yellow sputum, headache, nasal congestion, slight thirst.
- **Tongue/Pulse**: Reddened tongue tip/margins, thin yellow coating;
  floating rapid pulse.

### 2. Phlegm-Heat Obstructing the Lung (Peak/Core Stage)
- **Key Symptoms**: Rough cough, yellow viscous difficult-to-expectorate sputum,
  high fever (without chills), thirst with desire for cold drinks, chest
  oppression or pain, yellow urine.
- **Tongue/Pulse**: Red tongue body, yellow greasy coating; slippery rapid pulse.

### 3. Heat Invading the Pericardium (Critical Variant)
- **Key Symptoms**: Persistent high fever, clouded consciousness with delirium,
  agitation, possible convulsions.
- **Tongue/Pulse**: Deep-red tongue, dry yellow coating; thready rapid pulse.

## Standard Inquiry Logic

Respiratory specialty basic inquiry → Etiology (external contraction vs.
internal injury) inquiry → Core cough & sputum characteristics inquiry →
Lung & systemic accompanying symptom inquiry → Throat, lung sign, and imaging
verification → Lung-Spleen-Liver-Kidney zang-fu pathomechanism inquiry →
Respiratory disease-specific differentiation → Critical condition and
complication screening → External/internal injury cough syndrome differentiation →
TCM respiratory disease diagnosis confirmation.

## Risk Screening

- **Respiratory Failure Risk**: Progressive dyspnea, cyanosis, decreased SpO₂
- **Sepsis Risk**: High fever with chills, hypotension, altered mental status
- **Underlying Malignancy**: Unexplained weight loss, hemoptysis, imaging
  showing mass lesions — flag for further oncological evaluation
