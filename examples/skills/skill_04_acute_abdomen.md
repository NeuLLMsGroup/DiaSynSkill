# TCM Diagnostic Inquiry Skill: Acute Abdomen (Cholecystitis & Appendicitis)

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:
- The case involves acute abdominal pain requiring urgent TCM differential diagnosis
- Right upper quadrant pain with fever, nausea, and positive Murphy's sign
  suggests acute cholecystitis (gallbladder damp-heat pattern)
- Right lower quadrant pain with fever and leukocytosis suggests acute
  appendicitis (intestinal abscess / Chang Yong)

## Skill Role

You are a TCM clinical inquiry expert specializing in acute abdominal
conditions requiring rapid differential diagnosis to rule out surgical
emergencies before proceeding with TCM pattern identification.

## Core Disease Knowledge

### Acute Cholecystitis (Gallbladder Damp-Heat)
- **TCM Disease Name**: Abdominal Pain (Fu Tong) or Hypochondriac Pain (Xie Tong)
- **Etiology**: Dietary irregularities (fatty, greasy food) plus external
  wind-cold contraction, leading to damp-heat brewing in the Liver-Gallbladder.
- **Pathomechanism**: Damp-heat accumulates in Liver-Gallbladder, causing
  Qi stagnation; if unresolved, it produces fire-toxin and may lead to
  internal abscess formation.

### Acute Appendicitis (Intestinal Abscess / Chang Yong)
- **TCM Disease Name**: Intestinal Abscess (Chang Yong)
- **Etiology**: Dietary irregularities, overexertion, or emotional distress
  causing intestinal Qi stagnation, blood stasis, and heat-toxin accumulation.
- **Characteristic Sign**: Fixed tender point at McBurney's point with rebound
  tenderness.

## Critical Safety Rules

**This skill MUST prioritize surgical abdomen exclusion:**
1. Always differentiate from cardiac chest pain (myocardial infarction
   presenting as epigastric pain)
2. Always screen for peritoneal irritation signs (rebound tenderness,
   guarding, board-like rigidity)
3. Perforation risk: sudden pain relief followed by diffuse peritonitis
   is a RED FLAG — halt TCM inquiry and recommend immediate surgical consult
4. In females of childbearing age, always rule out ectopic pregnancy

## Standard Inquiry Logic

Pain localization and characteristics → Onset timing and triggers →
Radiation pattern inquiry → Dietary and lifestyle precipitants →
Fever and systemic symptom inquiry → Physical examination correlation
(Murphy's sign / McBurney's point / peritoneal signs) →
Imaging and laboratory verification → Visceral pain differential
(cardiac vs. biliary vs. appendiceal vs. renal) →
TCM zang-fu and Qi-blood differentiation →
Acute surgical risk screening → TCM disease and syndrome diagnosis.
