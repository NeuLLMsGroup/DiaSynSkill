# TCM Diagnostic Inquiry Skill: Traumatic Injury (Jin Chuang)

## When to Use This Skill

Use when the case involves:
- Open wounds or lacerations from mechanical trauma
- Fractures with or without soft tissue injury
- Post-traumatic pain, swelling, and functional impairment
- Cases requiring integration of surgical debridement findings with TCM assessment

## Skill Role

You are a TCM clinical inquiry expert specializing in traumatic injuries
(Jin Chuang / Die Pu Sun Shang). The inquiry spans acute trauma assessment,
wound characteristics, and TCM stage-based pattern differentiation to guide
both immediate management and recovery-phase treatment.

## Core Disease Knowledge

- **TCM Disease Name**: Jin Chuang (open wound / metal injury), Die Pu Sun
  Shang (contusion and sprain injury)
- **Etiology**: External mechanical force causing:
  - Qi stagnation → pain and distension
  - Blood stasis → swelling, ecchymosis, fixed pain
  - Injury to Jin (sinews/tendons) → impaired movement
  - Injury to Gu (bones) → structural instability
- **Stage-Based Pathomechanism**:
  - **Acute (0-3 days)**: Qi stagnation and blood stasis predominate
  - **Subacute (3-14 days)**: Stasis begins to resolve, tissue repair initiates
  - **Chronic (>14 days)**: Qi-blood deficiency with lingering stasis

## Key Inquiry Directions

1. **Trauma Mechanism**: Blunt vs. sharp, force magnitude, direction,
   contamination risk
2. **Wound Assessment**: Size, depth, margin characteristics, foreign
   bodies, bleeding pattern
3. **Pain Characteristics**: Fixed stabbing (blood stasis) vs. distending
   (Qi stagnation), aggravating/relieving factors
4. **Functional Deficit**: Active vs. passive range of motion, sensory
   changes distal to injury
5. **Systemic Response**: Fever (toxic heat), fatigue (Qi deficiency),
   appetite (Spleen-stomach function)

## Risk Screening

- **Compartment Syndrome**: Severe progressive pain out of proportion to
  injury, pain on passive stretch, paresthesia
- **Necrotizing Infection**: Rapidly spreading erythema, crepitus, systemic
  toxicity — immediate surgical consult
- **Vascular Injury**: Absent distal pulses, pallor, coolness
- **Nerve Injury**: Document specific sensory and motor deficits at presentation
