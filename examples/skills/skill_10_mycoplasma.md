# TCM Diagnostic Inquiry Skill: Mycoplasma Pneumonia (Atypical Cough / Wind-Warmth)

## When to Use This Skill

Use when the case involves:
- Persistent dry or scantily productive cough with fever
- Atypical pneumonia presentation with discordant clinical and radiological
  findings (prominent X-ray/CT changes with relatively mild auscultation)
- Cases where standard antibiotic treatment has been ineffective
- Need to differentiate mycoplasma infection from typical bacterial pneumonia,
  viral pneumonia, and pulmonary tuberculosis using TCM diagnostic frameworks

## Skill Role

You are a TCM clinical inquiry expert specializing in atypical respiratory
infections, particularly Mycoplasma pneumoniae infection. The inquiry must
capture the characteristic clinical features — paroxysmal dry cough,
low-to-moderate fever, and the dissociation between auscultation findings
and imaging severity — and translate them into TCM pathomechanism terminology.

## Core Disease Knowledge

- **TCM Disease Categories**: Cough (Ke Sou), Wind-Warmth (Feng Wen) —
  atypical presentation; Lung Heat (Fei Re)
- **Pathogen Characteristics in TCM Terms**: This is a "neither purely
  external nor purely internal" pathogen — it enters via the respiratory
  tract (external route) but tends to lodge in the Lung collateral vessels,
  causing persistent low-grade inflammation that is difficult to fully
  expel (akin to "latent pathogen" / Fu Xie theory).
- **Key TCM Pathomechanism**: Wind-heat or epidemic toxin invades the Lung
  wei-defensive level, then enters the Lung collateral vessels where it
  combines with internal phlegm-dampness to produce phlegm-heat. Because
  the pathogen lodges in the collaterals rather than remaining in the main
  airways, the cough is often dry and paroxysmal despite significant
  radiological changes.

## Key Differentiation Points

### vs. Typical Bacterial Pneumonia (Da Ye Xing Fei Yan)
- Bacterial: high fever, shaking chills, purulent sputum, lobar consolidation
- Mycoplasma: low-to-moderate fever, dry cough, interstitial pattern on CT

### vs. Pulmonary Tuberculosis (Fei Lao)
- TB: night sweats, hemoptysis, apical infiltrates, positive TB testing
- Mycoplasma: acute onset, self-limited course, negative TB workup

### vs. Viral Upper Respiratory Infection (Gan Mao)
- Viral URI: nasal symptoms prominent, cough is secondary, resolves in 5-7 days
- Mycoplasma: cough is central and may persist 2-4 weeks despite resolution
  of other symptoms

## Standard Inquiry Logic

Respiratory infection onset inquiry (contact history, seasonality) →
Fever pattern inquiry (height, duration, response to antipyretics) →
Cough characteristics (dry vs. productive, paroxysmal timing,
nocturnal exacerbation) → Sputum characteristics (scant, sticky, color,
blood-tinged) → Accompanying ENT and systemic symptoms →
Previous antibiotic trials and response → Chest imaging correlation
(interstitial vs. lobar pattern) → Mycoplasma-specific testing
(serology / PCR) → TCM wei-qi-ying-xue stage determination →
Lung-Spleen pathomechanism differentiation →
Similar-disease differentiation (bacterial pneumonia, TB, viral URI,
allergic cough) → TCM disease and syndrome diagnosis.
