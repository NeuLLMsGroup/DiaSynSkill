# Dialogue Example 10: Mycoplasma Pneumonia (Atypical Cough)

**Disease**: Mycoplasma Pneumoniae Infection (Atypical Pneumonia — Wind-Warmth / Cough)
**Department**: Respiratory Medicine / Infectious Disease
**Syndrome Type**: Wind-Heat Invading Lung with Latent Phlegm-Heat in Collaterals
**Dialogue Rounds**: 13

---

**Patient**: Doctor, I've been coughing for two weeks. It started with a mild fever and sore throat, but now it's just this persistent dry cough that won't go away. My chest X-ray shows pneumonia but the antibiotics didn't help.

**Doctor**: Tell me more about this cough. Is it completely dry or do you have any sputum? When does it get worse?

**Patient**: Mostly dry, but sometimes I manage to bring up a tiny bit of sticky white phlegm. It's worst at night — I'll have these coughing fits that last several minutes and leave me exhausted.

**Doctor**: What antibiotics did you take and for how long?

**Patient**: Amoxicillin for 5 days — no improvement at all. Then the doctor switched me to azithromycin and I started feeling a bit better after 3 days.

**Doctor**: That's an important clue — amoxicillin doesn't work against mycoplasma because it lacks a cell wall. The response to azithromycin supports a mycoplasma diagnosis. Did you have a mycoplasma antibody test?

**Patient**: Yes — the IgM was positive.

**Doctor**: Now that we know it's mycoplasma pneumonia, let me ask you some more detailed questions from a TCM perspective. When these coughing fits happen, do you feel like there's something stuck in your throat that you can't clear?

**Patient**: Yes! Exactly — it feels like there's a tickle or something lodged deep in my throat, and I just can't stop coughing until that sensation goes away.

**【Diagnostic Summary】**
- **TCM Disease Diagnosis**: Cough (Ke Sou) due to Wind-Heat Invading Lung with Latent Pathogen Lodging in Collaterals
- **Core Syndrome Type**: Wind-Heat with Phlegm-Heat in Lung Collaterals (atypical pathogen — neither fully external nor fully internal)
- **Key Evidence**: (1) Acute onset with fever and sore throat — wind-heat invading the lung wei-defensive level; (2) Persistent paroxysmal dry cough for 2 weeks, worse at night — pathogen has lodged in lung collaterals; (3) Scant sticky sputum, sensation of something stuck in throat — phlegm-heat in collaterals; (4) No response to beta-lactam antibiotics, positive mycoplasma IgM, response to macrolide — confirms atypical pathogen; (5) CT/X-ray shows interstitial pattern disproportionate to auscultation findings — characteristic of mycoplasma
- **Similar-Disease Differentiation**:
  - vs. Typical Bacterial Pneumonia: No high fever with chills, no purulent sputum, no lobar consolidation on imaging, no response to amoxicillin
  - vs. Pulmonary Tuberculosis: Acute onset (not insidious), no night sweats or hemoptysis, negative TB workup
  - vs. Allergic Cough / CVA: No atopic history, positive infectious serology, imaging shows pneumonia (not just hyperinflation)
- **TCM Pathomechanism Insight**: This pathogen demonstrates characteristics of "latent pathogen" (Fu Xie) theory — it enters via the external route (respiratory tract) but lodges in the lung collateral vessels where it is difficult to fully expel, causing persistent low-grade inflammation that manifests as paroxysmal dry cough. The key therapeutic principle is to both release the exterior (for the residual wind-heat) and resolve phlegm-stasis in the collaterals (for the lodged pathogen).
- **Risk Screening**: Currently afebrile, SpO₂ normal; cough gradually improving on appropriate antibiotics; no respiratory distress; complete resolution expected in 2-4 weeks
