# Dialogue Example 7: Chronic Bronchitis with Acute Exacerbation

**Disease**: Chronic Bronchitis (Chronic Cough — Phlegm-Dampness in Lung)
**Department**: Respiratory Medicine
**Syndrome Type**: Spleen Deficiency Generating Phlegm-Dampness, External Wind-Cold Triggering Acute Exacerbation
**Dialogue Rounds**: 12

---

**Patient**: Doctor, I cough every winter — it's been like this for years. But this time it's worse. More phlegm, thicker, and I feel short of breath.

**Doctor**: How many years has this been going on? How many months does it last each winter? Have you been diagnosed with chronic bronchitis before?

**Patient**: At least 10-12 years. Every winter from November to March. Yes, the doctor said it's chronic bronchitis.

**Doctor**: Tell me about the sputum this time compared to usual. Color, amount, consistency — how is it different?

**Patient**: Usually it's white and frothy — I can cough it up in the morning. This time it's yellow-greenish, thicker, harder to bring up, and there's more of it.

**Doctor**: Do you smoke? If so, how much and for how long?

**Patient**: I smoked for 30 years — about a pack a day. I quit 5 years ago.

**Doctor**: Any wheezing or whistling sound when you breathe? Do you feel like you can't get enough air?

**Patient**: I can hear myself wheeze sometimes, especially at night. Walking uphill makes me breathe hard.

**Doctor**: How is your appetite? Do you feel heavy or tired easily? Any bloating after eating?

**Patient**: Appetite is reduced. I do feel tired and heavy — my body feels sluggish. Sometimes I get bloated.

**【Diagnostic Summary】**
- **TCM Disease Diagnosis**: Chronic Cough (Jiu Ke) — Acute Exacerbation
- **Core Syndrome Type**: Spleen Deficiency with Phlegm-Dampness (root) triggered by External Wind-Cold transforming into Phlegm-Heat (branch)
- **Key Evidence**: (1) Decades-long winter cough, white frothy sputum at baseline — Spleen deficiency generating phlegm; (2) Acute change to yellow-green thick sputum with increased volume — external pathogen transforming into heat; (3) Fatigue, heavy body sensation, bloating, poor appetite — Spleen Qi deficiency signs; (4) Smoking history — chronic airway damage
- **Similar-Disease Differentiation**: vs. COPD — no significant emphysema on imaging, FEV1/FVC not below 0.70; vs. Asthma — wheezing is mild and infection-triggered rather than allergic/atopic
- **Risk Screening**: Monitor for progression to COPD; annual spirometry recommended; smoking cessation maintained
