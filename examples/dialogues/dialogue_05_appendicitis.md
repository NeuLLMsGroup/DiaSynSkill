# Dialogue Example 5: Acute Appendicitis (Intestinal Abscess)

**Disease**: Acute Appendicitis (Intestinal Abscess — Chang Yong)
**Department**: General Surgery
**Syndrome Type**: Damp-Heat Stagnation with Qi-Blood Stasis in the Intestine
**Dialogue Rounds**: 12

---

**Patient**: Doctor, my stomach hurts — it started around my belly button yesterday and now it's moved to the lower right side. It's getting worse.

**Doctor**: When did this start exactly? Can you describe how the pain moved? Was it sudden or gradual?

**Patient**: Yesterday afternoon it started as a dull ache around my navel. By evening it had moved to the right lower side and became much sharper. I couldn't sleep well.

**Doctor**: Any nausea or vomiting? Have you had a fever?

**Patient**: I felt nauseous and threw up once. I checked my temperature — it was 37.8°C.

**Doctor**: Have you had any bowel movements? Any diarrhea or constipation?

**Patient**: I haven't had a bowel movement since yesterday. I feel bloated.

**Doctor**: Let me examine your abdomen. Lie down and relax. Does this hurt? [Palpating McBurney's point]

**Patient**: Yes! That's very tender.

**Doctor**: When I press and then release quickly — is the pain worse when I let go?

**Patient**: Yes, it hurts more when you let go.

**Doctor**: Here is the complete assessment.

**【Diagnostic Summary】**
- **TCM Disease Diagnosis**: Intestinal Abscess (Chang Yong) — Damp-Heat Stagnation with Qi-Blood Stasis
- **Core Syndrome Type**: Intestinal Damp-Heat Accumulation, Qi Stagnation and Blood Stasis (pre-suppuration stage)
- **Key Differentiation Evidence**:
  1. Periumbilical pain migrating to right lower quadrant with fixed tenderness — classic Chang Yong pattern
  2. Rebound tenderness at McBurney's point — peritoneal irritation, indicating progression beyond pure Qi stagnation
  3. Low-grade fever (37.8°C), nausea with vomiting, constipation — damp-heat accumulating in the intestine
  4. Acute onset, no prior similar episodes
- **Similar-Disease Differentiation**:
  - vs. Urinary Stone (Shi Lin / Urolithiasis): Pain typically radiates to groin; urinary symptoms (dysuria, hematuria) prominent — not present here
  - vs. Gynecological Acute Abdomen (in female patients): Ectopic pregnancy, ovarian torsion, PID — all require specific gynecological history and examination; ruled out where applicable
- **Risk Screening**:
  - **Perforation Risk**: Currently no diffuse peritonitis; monitor for sudden pain relief followed by diffuse severe pain — would indicate perforation requiring emergency surgery
  - Currently hemodynamically stable; surgical consultation obtained
- **Multimodal Data Verification**:
  - Bloodwork: Leukocytosis with neutrophil predominance — confirms acute inflammation
  - Ultrasound: Recommended to assess appendix diameter, presence of appendicolith, and periappendiceal fluid
