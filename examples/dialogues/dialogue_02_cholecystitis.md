# Dialogue Example 2: Acute Cholecystitis (Gallbladder Damp-Heat)

**Disease**: Acute Cholecystitis (Abdominal Pain — Liver-Gallbladder Damp-Heat)
**Department**: Gastroenterology
**Syndrome Type**: Liver-Gallbladder Damp-Heat Syndrome
**Dialogue Rounds**: 14

---

**Patient**: Doctor, my stomach has been hurting since this morning. It's pretty severe, and I feel feverish and weak all over.

**Doctor**: Can you point to exactly where the pain is? Upper abdomen, around the navel, or more on the right side?

**Patient**: Mainly the upper right side, under my ribs. It's constant, hasn't let up.

**Doctor**: How long has it been hurting? What kind of pain is it — distending, cramping, or stabbing?

**Patient**: About three hours now. It's constant but sometimes suddenly gets worse, like someone's twisting inside.

**Doctor**: Did you eat anything unusual before this started? Rich or greasy food? Any alcohol?

**Patient**: I had guests over yesterday and cooked a big meal. Had quite a bit of braised pork and some beer.

**Doctor**: Does the pain radiate anywhere — your right shoulder or back?

**Patient**: Yes, my right shoulder feels sore and my back is uncomfortable too.

**Doctor**: Have you had an ultrasound?

**Patient**: Yes — the doctor said my gallbladder wall is thickened.

**Doctor**: [Looking at the ultrasound report] The ultrasound shows gallbladder wall thickening with a distended gallbladder but no obvious stones, consistent with acute cholecystitis.

**Doctor**: Here is the complete diagnostic summary.

**【Diagnostic Summary】**
- **TCM Disease Diagnosis**: Abdominal Pain (Fu Tong) — Liver-Gallbladder Damp-Heat Syndrome
- **Core Syndrome Type**: Liver-Gallbladder Damp-Heat
- **Key Differentiation Evidence**:
  1. Right upper quadrant persistent pain with paroxysmal exacerbations, tenderness with guarding — characteristic of gallbladder Qi stagnation
  2. Fever (38°C), bitter taste, nausea with bilious vomiting, deep yellow urine — damp-heat signs
  3. Dietary trigger: overindulgence in fatty, greasy food plus external wind contraction
  4. Murphy's sign positive, peritoneal irritation signs present
  5. Ultrasound: gallbladder wall thickening — objective confirmation
- **Similar-Disease Differentiation**:
  - vs. Cardiac Pain (True Heart Pain): No chest tightness, palpitations, or left arm radiation — excluded
  - vs. Epigastric Pain (Wei Wantong): Pain is right subcostal, not epigastric; accompanied by gallbladder-specific signs
- **Risk Screening**: Currently hemodynamically stable (BP 120/70, HR 71). Monitor for: chills with high fever spikes, deepening jaundice, hypotension, altered consciousness — signs of ascending cholangitis or gallbladder perforation
- **Multimodal Data Verification**: Ultrasound confirmed gallbladder wall thickening without calculi, consistent with clinical presentation
