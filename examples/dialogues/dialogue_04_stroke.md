# Dialogue Example 4: Stroke with Qi-Yin Deficiency and Blood Stasis

**Disease**: Stroke (Zhongfeng — Meridian Pattern)
**Department**: Neurology
**Syndrome Type**: Qi-Yin Deficiency with Blood Stasis Obstructing Collaterals
**Dialogue Rounds**: 14

---

**Patient**: Doctor, I suddenly had trouble speaking two months ago, and the right side of my body became weak. It's not getting better.

**Doctor**: When exactly did this happen? Were you doing anything specific at the time? Did you lose consciousness?

**Patient**: It happened suddenly one morning when I woke up — my speech was slurred and my right limbs were weak. I didn't lose consciousness.

**Doctor**: What medical attention did you receive at that time? Have you had a brain CT or MRI?

**Patient**: I went to the emergency room and they did an MRI. They said it was a stroke — a cerebral infarction. I was hospitalized for two weeks.

**Doctor**: [Reviewing MRI report] I can see the MRI shows multiple ischemic infarcts with encephalomalacia, consistent with the stroke diagnosis. Let me now ask you some detailed questions about your current condition and overall constitution.

**Doctor**: Here is the complete diagnostic summary.

**【Diagnostic Summary】**
- **TCM Disease Diagnosis**: Stroke (Zhongfeng — Meridian Pattern)
- **Core Syndrome Type**: Qi-Yin Deficiency with Blood Stasis Obstructing Collaterals
- **Key Differentiation Evidence**:
  1. Sudden onset hemiplegia and speech difficulty with clear consciousness — Zhongfeng, meridian pattern
  2. Fixed stabbing pain in affected limbs, dark-red tongue with stasis spots — blood stasis
  3. Fatigue, shortness of breath on exertion, spontaneous sweating — Qi deficiency
  4. Dry mouth and throat, lumbar soreness, night sweats — Yin deficiency
  5. MRI: multiple ischemic infarcts and encephalomalacia — objective imaging correlation
- **Similar-Disease Differentiation**:
  - vs. Bi Syndrome: No migratory joint pain or weather-sensitivity; MRI confirms brain lesion
  - vs. Wei Syndrome: Not purely flaccid; numbness and pain present plus imaging evidence
- **Risk Screening**: Currently stable; secondary prevention of recurrent stroke is the priority. Monitor for: sudden worsening of neurological deficits, new-onset atrial fibrillation, uncontrolled blood pressure
