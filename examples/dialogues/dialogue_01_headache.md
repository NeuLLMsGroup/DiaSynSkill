# Dialogue Example 1: Intracranial Mass Presenting as Progressive Headache

**Disease**: True Headache (Intracranial Space-Occupying Lesion)
**Department**: Neurology / Neurosurgery
**Syndrome Type**: Phlegm-Stasis Mutual Binding, Obstructing Brain Collaterals
**Dialogue Rounds**: 15

---

**Patient**: Doctor, I've had headaches for the past two months, along with nausea and vomiting. I've also been unsteady on my feet — it's getting worse. Can you help me?

**Doctor**: Hello, please have a seat. When did the headaches start? Was there a specific trigger or did they come on gradually?

**Patient**: About two months ago. At first I didn't think much of it — just a dull ache. But it's been getting progressively worse. No particular trigger, it just started suddenly.

**Doctor**: Have you had any head trauma in the past couple of months? Has work been stressful? Any emotional issues — irritability or depression? How's your diet — do you eat a lot of rich, greasy foods?

**Patient**: No head injury. Work has been pretty busy and stressful, and I do get frustrated sometimes. As for food, I have a lot of business dinners, so I do eat quite a bit of greasy food.

**Doctor**: Do you have any chronic conditions like hypertension or diabetes? Are you on any long-term medications?

**Patient**: I've always been pretty healthy. No major illnesses and no long-term medications.

**Doctor**: Let me now summarize the complete diagnostic assessment.

**【Diagnostic Summary】**
- **TCM Disease Diagnosis**: True Headache (Zhen Toutong)
- **Core Syndrome Type**: Phlegm-Stasis Mutual Binding, Obstructing Brain Collaterals
- **Key Differentiation Evidence**:
  1. Persistent headache for 2 months with progressive worsening, fixed location in the right temporal region — consistent with blood stasis obstructing collaterals
  2. Projectile vomiting without post-emesis relief, heavy head sensation, sticky sputum, poor appetite, abdominal distension — phlegm-turbidity ascending to disturb the clear orifices
  3. Left upper limb weakness (Grade III) and gait instability — brain collaterals obstructed, impairing motor function
  4. CT/MRI: Right temporal lobe mass (≈6.0×5.8×5.0 cm) with calcifications and perilesional edema — consistent with phlegm-stasis congealing into a mass
  5. Irritability, bitter taste, poor sleep — secondary phlegm-stasis transforming into heat, disturbing the mind
- **Similar-Disease Differentiation**:
  - vs. Migraine: Migraine is episodic, unilateral throbbing, with photophobia/phonophobia, relieved by rest. This case is continuous and progressive with objective neurological deficits — excluded.
  - vs. External Contraction Headache: Acute onset with chills, fever, and nasal symptoms. No such history here — excluded.
  - vs. Tension Headache: Bilateral band-like pressure, mild intensity, no progression or intracranial mass. Excluded.
- **Risk Screening**:
  - **Intracranial Hypertension Risk**: Progressive headache with frequent projectile vomiting — HIGH RISK
  - **Brain Herniation Risk**: Currently alert, pupils equal and reactive. But large mass with significant edema — requires close monitoring
  - **Red Flags for Emergency**: Sudden explosive headache worsening, deteriorating consciousness, unilateral pupil dilation, seizures
- **Multimodal Data Verification**:
  - CT: Confirmed right temporal irregular hyperdense mass with calcifications and edema
  - MRI: Confirmed right temporal abnormal signal with mild heterogeneous enhancement
  - Funduscopy and EEG: Not yet performed — recommend to complete
