# Dialogue Example 9: Traumatic Hand Injury (Jin Chuang)

**Disease**: Open Right Hand Injury with Fracture and Tendon Injury (Jin Chuang)
**Department**: Orthopedics / Hand Surgery
**Syndrome Type**: Acute Qi Stagnation and Blood Stasis (early stage)
**Dialogue Rounds**: 11

---

**Patient**: Doctor, I cut my hand at work — it's bleeding quite a bit and I can't move my fingers properly.

**Doctor**: Let me see. How did this happen? What exactly caused the injury?

**Patient**: I was using a power saw and it slipped. It cut into the back of my right hand.

**Doctor**: How long ago did this happen? Have you applied any first aid?

**Patient**: About 30 minutes ago. My coworker wrapped it with a clean cloth and we came straight here.

**Doctor**: [Examining the wound] I can see a laceration approximately 6 cm on the dorsal aspect of the right hand, extending from the second to fourth metacarpal region. There appears to be tendon involvement. Can you try to extend your fingers for me?

**Patient**: [Attempts extension] I can't lift my middle and ring fingers — they won't move.

**Doctor**: Any numbness or tingling in the fingers? Can you feel this? [Testing sensation]

**Patient**: I can feel light touch on all fingers. There's no numbness — just pain.

**【Diagnostic Summary】**
- **TCM Disease Diagnosis**: Jin Chuang (Open Wound with Tendon and Bone Injury) — Right Hand
- **Core Syndrome Type**: Acute Qi Stagnation and Blood Stasis (early trauma stage)
- **Key Evidence**: (1) Sharp instrument injury with open wound — external mechanical damage; (2) Loss of active finger extension (middle and ring fingers) — tendon (Jin) injury; (3) Localized severe pain, swelling beginning — Qi stagnation and blood stasis; (4) Intact sensation distally — major nerve trunks spared
- **TCM Staging**: Currently in acute stage (0-3 days) — Qi stagnation and blood stasis predominate; anticipate subacute transition in 3-14 days with blood stasis resolution and tissue repair; chronic stage after 14 days may involve Qi-blood deficiency if healing is delayed
- **Risk Screening**: No compartment syndrome signs; distal pulses intact; no necrotizing infection risk at presentation; tetanus prophylaxis indicated (check immunization status); surgical exploration and repair recommended for tendon and possible metacarpal involvement
